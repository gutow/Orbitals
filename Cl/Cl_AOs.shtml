Visualization of atomic orbitals on Cl atom

This page is designed to allow you to manipulate images of atomic orbitals and compare multiple orbitals by displaying them simultaneously on one atom. The goal is to help you visualize atomic orbitals and their relative sizes.

Below (you may need to scroll down) is a box with a green dot in it. The green dot represents the location of a chlorine nucleus (significantly enlarged so that you can see it). To the right (or below on a narrow screen) are dropdown selectors from which you can choose an orbital to display and control the appearance of each orbital. The orbitals appear in the dropdowns from lowest (at top) to highest (at bottom) energy.

The electronic configuration of a ground state chlorine is [Ne]3s23p5 (1s22s22p63s23p5).  This means that the 4s, 3d and 4p orbitals shown are unoccupied;  thus their extent and energy are somewhat different than would be found for orbitals with electrons in them.

  • How these orbital images are generated

    These orbital images are based on calculations done using the GAMESS1 ab initio quantum mechanics package. These images are generated on the fly by JSmol/Jmol from the GAMESS.log file of a single point energy calculation on a ground state Cl at the 6-311G++(2d,p) level of theory.
